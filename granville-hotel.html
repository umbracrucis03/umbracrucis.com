Granville Hotel

STORY 1 • ENGLISH TALES • WRITTEN BY UMBRA CRUCIS

Radhika was travelling from Lucknow to Kolkata for a new business deal. It had already become quite late at night, and she was extremely tired. So, she turned to her driver, Kotwaal, and said, “Stop the car wherever you find a hotel nearby.”

“Yes, madam,” Kotwaal replied. A short while later, he pulled over in front of an old, imposing structure standing beside the highway.

Radhika rolled down the car window and looked at the building. The hotel stood almost in the middle of nowhere, surrounded by complete darkness. There was no sign of human life anywhere in sight—no nearby houses, no shops, no passing vehicles. Nothing.

Exhausted and eager to rest, she asked Kotwaal to take her luggage out of the car. Carrying a trolley in one hand and her purse in the other, Radhika stepped inside.

At the front desk, she booked a room for four days and completed the check-in formalities. The manager handed her the key to Room Number 336 and said softly, “Madam, I have one request. Please stay away from Room Number 333, which is directly opposite your room. Have a good night!”

Something about his voice felt off, but Radhika was far too tired to care. She simply nodded, took the lift upstairs, unlocked Room 336, and collapsed onto the bed. Meanwhile, Kotwaal decided to sleep inside the car outside.

The First Glimpse

The next morning around 8:00 AM, Radhika got ready for her business meeting. As she locked her door, she glanced at Room 333 directly opposite hers. It looked completely quiet. Running late, she brushed off the manager's warning and rushed out.

After a long day, Radhika returned through the quiet corridor, lightheartedly humming to herself. As she reached her room, her eyes drifted back to Room 333. She couldn't help but walk closer this time.

The door looked ancient, sealed with three large padlocks that were tightly bound together with thick red threads. Staring at it, a wave of curiosity washed over her. What could possibly be hidden inside?

Her eyes settled on the tiny keyhole. Bending down slowly, she pressed one eye against the opening.

Inside, bathed in faint light, sat a woman on a chair. Her long hair was loose, draped over a plain white saree. She was facing away from the door, keeping her face hidden. Unsettled but tired, Radhika backed away and went into her room to sleep.

3:13 A.M.

The next night passed peacefully until precisely 3:13 AM. Radhika suddenly snapped awake to a faint, eerie melody floating through the room. Someone was softly humming... accompanied by the low, sorrowful sound of a violin.

“Who could be playing music at this hour?” she whispered. Slipping out of bed, she tiptoed into the hallway and tracked the sound straight to Room 333.

Once again, she leaned in and peered through the keyhole. The woman was still sitting on the chair with her back to the door, but now her arms moved gracefully as she played the violin, humming that haunting tune.

Chills ran down Radhika's spine. She resolved to complain to the manager first thing in the morning and retreated back into her room.

Everything Was Red

Morning arrived—her final day at the hotel. After packing her bags, Radhika stepped out into the hallway. The memory of the violin music hit her, and she paused in front of Room 333 one last time.

Leaning down, she pressed her eye to the keyhole again. But this time, there was no room, no chair, and no woman. Everything was pitch-red. A solid, suffocating shade of crimson filled her entire field of vision.

Baffled and annoyed, she grabbed her luggage, went down to the reception, and confronted the manager. “What is going on with Room 333? First, a woman in white, then violin music at 3 AM, and today when I looked through the keyhole, everything inside was completely red!”

The manager's expression drained of all color. He looked at her with grave, hollow eyes.

“You are right, madam,” he said quietly. “There is the shadow of a witch trapped inside that room.”

Radhika stared at him, speechless.

“And today,” the manager whispered, “when you looked through the keyhole... she was looking right back at you with her blood-red eyes.”

A horrific realization hit Radhika. Her vision blurred, her pulse stopped, and she collapsed onto the lobby floor. By the time Kotwaal rushed inside to check on her, her body was already ice-cold.

The manager stood silently behind the counter. With a faint, sinister smile, he pulled out his guest register... and quietly drew a dark line across Radhika's name.
